Heart 101

The basics, without the scare tactics

Cardiovascular disease is the world's leading cause of death — and a lot of the risk is built from everyday habits. Here's what's worth knowing before you're 30, and what's actually in your hands.

The system

What "cardiovascular" means

Your heart plus every blood vessel it pumps through — arteries, veins, capillaries. Together they move oxygen, fuel and heat around your body roughly 100,000 heartbeats a day.

The slow build

How disease develops

Cholesterol, inflammation and pressure gradually stiffen and narrow arteries (atherosclerosis). It's usually silent for decades — which is exactly why early habits matter.

Risk factors

What raises risk — split by what you can change

In your hands

Modifiable

Physical inactivity · smoking and vaping · diet high in salt, sugar and ultra-processed food · excess alcohol · poor sleep · chronic stress · unmanaged high blood pressure, cholesterol or blood sugar.

Good to know

Non-modifiable

Family history of early heart disease · age · some ethnic backgrounds carry higher baseline risk · certain inherited conditions. You can't change these, but knowing them tells you how early to pay attention.

The levers

Six things that genuinely move the needle

1. Move most days

Aim toward 150 min/week of moderate activity. Even short bouts count. Sitting less matters on its own.

2. Don't smoke or vape

Nicotine raises heart rate and blood pressure and damages vessel linings. Quitting benefits start within weeks.

3. Eat more plants

More fibre, less salt and less ultra-processed food. No perfect diet required — direction beats perfection.

4. Protect sleep

7–9 hours, regular timing. Short and broken sleep pushes blood pressure and appetite the wrong way.

5. Manage stress

Chronic stress is a real risk factor. Movement, social contact and time outdoors all lower the load.

6. Know your numbers

Blood pressure, cholesterol, blood sugar, resting heart rate. Get a baseline — many campuses check for free.

Myth check

Things people your age get told

"Heart problems are an old-person thing."

The events (heart attacks, strokes) cluster later in life, but the process starts young. Early arterial changes are common in people in their twenties. Prevention is a long game you're already playing.

"I'm skinny, so my heart's fine."

Body size is only one signal. Blood pressure, cholesterol, fitness, smoking and blood sugar all affect risk independently of weight. You can look lean and still have numbers worth checking.

"Cardio means running, and I hate running."

Anything that raises your heart rate for a sustained stretch works: cycling, dancing, football, brisk walking, swimming, hiking. The best cardio is the one you'll keep doing.

"Energy drinks are basically coffee."

Many contain 150–300mg caffeine plus sugar and other stimulants, often drunk fast. High doses can trigger palpitations and raise blood pressure, especially mixed with alcohol or on no sleep.

"Vaping is fine for your heart."

It's newer, so long-term data is still building, but nicotine itself acutely raises heart rate and blood pressure and stiffens arteries. "Less bad than cigarettes" is not the same as "no cardiovascular effect."

Know the signs

When to get help now, not later

Call your local emergency number for: chest pain or pressure that lasts more than a few minutes or spreads to the arm, jaw or back; sudden shortness of breath; fainting; a face droop, arm weakness or slurred speech (signs of stroke). Don't wait to "see if it passes."
